One plain sentence for each term the site uses.

Contact scores

RRCS (residue-residue contact score)
How tightly two residues pack in one structure, summed over their pairs of atoms within 4.63 Å of each other (closer pairs count more); 0 means no contact. Learn more
Active RRCS, Inactive RRCS
The RRCS of one contact pair in the active model and in the inactive model.
ΔRRCS (delta RRCS)
The change in one contact: its RRCS in the active model minus its RRCS in the inactive model. Positive (red) means stronger in the active model, negative (blue) stronger in the inactive model.
|ΔRRCS|, |Δ|
The size of a change whatever its direction: ΔRRCS without its sign.
Contact pair
Two residues with atoms within 4.63 Å of each other in at least one of the two models.
Threshold
The |ΔRRCS| a contact must reach to count as changed on a receptor's page: that receptor's mean |ΔRRCS| plus one standard deviation, and never below 0.2.
Magnitude (HIGH, MED, LOW)
How large a pair's change is: HIGH at |Δ| 5.0 or more, MED from the receptor's threshold up to 5.0, LOW below the threshold.
Active-favoring, inactive-favoring
A contact or residue that is stronger in the active model (red, positive ΔRRCS) or in the inactive model (blue, negative ΔRRCS).
Total |ΔRRCS|
|ΔRRCS| added up over all of a receptor's contact pairs; it grows with the number of contacts as well as with the size of their changes.
Signed sum, Residue net Δ
ΔRRCS added up over all contacts of one residue, keeping the signs: the net gain (red) or loss (blue) of contact at that residue.
Largest single contact, Residue max Δ
The one contact of a residue with the largest |ΔRRCS|, colored by that contact's sign.
Std Dev, σ (standard deviation)
How widely values spread around their mean.
Å (ångström)
A tenth of a nanometer; atoms in contact sit about 3 to 4.6 Å apart.

The models

Active model, inactive model
Two computer predictions of the same receptor, one in the active (signaling) state and one in the inactive state. They are models, not experimental structures.
AlphaFold-Multistate
A way of running AlphaFold that predicts a GPCR in a chosen activation state by giving it only template structures of that state (Heo and Feig, 2022). GPCRdb built most of the models here this way; each report names the method of its two models. Learn more
AlphaFold2
Standard AlphaFold2, run with template structures of any state (Jumper et al., 2021). GPCRdb classed each such model as active or inactive; plain AlphaFold2 built 165 of the inactive models and 26 of the active models here, and AlphaFold-Multistate built the rest. Each report names the method of its two models. Learn more

The receptor

GPCR (G protein-coupled receptor)
A cell-surface receptor that crosses the membrane seven times and signals through G proteins. Learn more
Class A
The largest GPCR class, related to rhodopsin; this database covers 283 human class A receptors.
GPCRdb
The reference database of G protein-coupled receptors; the source of the generic numbers, the segment boundaries and the snake plot layout. Learn more
Residue, Res1, Res2
One amino acid in the protein chain, written as its code and position, for example D130; Res1 and Res2 are the two residues of a contact pair.
AA (amino acid)
One of the 20 building blocks of a protein; AA1 and AA2 are the amino acids of a pair.
Segment
One stretch of the receptor: a transmembrane helix (TM1 to TM7), a loop between two helices (ICL inside the cell, ECL outside), helix 8 (H8) or one of the two ends (N-term, C-term).
TM, TM1 to TM7 (transmembrane helix)
The seven helices that cross the cell membrane, numbered from the N-terminus.
ICL, ECL (intracellular and extracellular loops)
The loops joining the helices, on the inside (ICL1 to ICL3) or the outside (ECL1 to ECL3) of the cell.
H8 (helix 8)
A short helix after TM7 that lies along the inner face of the membrane.
N-term, C-term (N-terminus, C-terminus)
The two ends of the chain, the N-terminus outside the cell and the C-terminus inside; often flexible, so their contacts are the least reliable part of a model.
Generic number (Ballesteros-Weinstein numbering, written 3.50x50 by GPCRdb)
A residue address shared across receptors. In 3.50x50, 3 is the helix (TM3) and 50 marks the helix's most conserved residue, so one number names the equivalent residue in every receptor; the number after the x is GPCRdb's structure-based correction. Learn more
Snake plot
A flat map of the receptor's sequence, one circle per residue, drawn as the chain threads back and forth through the membrane. Learn more
CFR (Core Functional Residue)
The term has two meanings. A receptor-level CFR is a residue of one receptor that takes part in a contact whose |ΔRRCS| is above that receptor's threshold. The class-wide CFR positions are generic positions where receptor-level CFRs recur in at least three receptors, ranked by CFR score. The site marks the top 50: the snake plots, the residue table and search call a residue a CFR when its generic number is one of the 50 top-ranked class-wide CFR positions (by CFR score), and the Statistics page lists them.
CFR-connected residue
A residue that takes part in a contact above its receptor's ΔRRCS threshold whose partner residue sits at a CFR position in the same receptor. A CFR position here is one of the 50 top-ranked class-wide CFR positions (by CFR score), the positions search and the snake plots mark as CFR.
Near a CFR
A residue within two residues of a CFR position along the chain, in the same segment (helix, loop or terminus). CFR positions are the same 50 top-ranked class-wide CFR positions (by CFR score).
In contact with a CFR, below threshold
A residue with a contact to a CFR residue in at least one of the two models (RRCS above 0) that stays below the receptor's threshold.
xMY motif
A TM3 sequence pattern: Met at 3.36 and Tyr at 3.37, after a variable residue at 3.35. Of the 283 Class A receptors, 30 carry it, the four PARs among them. Its two contacts, Met3.36 to Tyr3.37 and Met3.36 to the 6.48 toggle-switch position, tend to be weaker in the active model across the receptors that carry it.
Ligand type
The kind of molecule that naturally activates the receptor, for example an amine, a peptide or a lipid; orphan receptors have no confirmed natural ligand.

Variants

Missense variant
A DNA change that swaps one amino acid of the protein for another.
Contact-site variants
The gnomAD missense variants listed on a report: those at residues that take part in at least one contact pair with |ΔRRCS| ≥ 3.0, a fixed cutoff rather than the receptor threshold.
gnomAD (Genome Aggregation Database)
Genetic variation seen in hundreds of thousands of people; the source of the missense variants and their frequencies (gnomAD v4.1.2 (dataset gnomad_r4, accessed 18 October 2026)). Learn more
Allele frequency (AF)
The share of chromosomes in gnomAD that carry the variant; 1e-4 means 1 in 10,000.
Het count (heterozygote count)
How many people in gnomAD carry one copy of the variant (allele count minus twice the number of people who carry two copies).
Protein change, DNA change (HGVS notation)
The standard way to write a variant: p.Arg151Cys is the arginine at protein position 151 replaced by cysteine, and c.451C>T is the DNA letter change behind it.
dbSNP, rsID
A variant's identifier in dbSNP, NCBI's catalog of genetic variants, such as rs1042713.
AlphaMissense, AM
A machine-learning prediction of how likely one amino-acid change is to harm the protein, from 0 to 1. Likely pathogenic: score > 0.564. Ambiguous: 0.34 ≤ score ≤ 0.564. Likely benign: score < 0.34. Classes follow the source predictions before display rounding. A prediction, not a clinical classification. Learn more
Pathogenicity
AlphaMissense's class for a change: Benign, Ambiguous or Pathogenic, from its score.
Conservation
How unchanged a position is across related proteins, from 0 (variable) to 1 (conserved); conserved positions are often important (VarSite scores, served by ProtVar). Learn more

Statistics page

CFR score
The mean of a position's normalized recurrence (how many receptors) and normalized mean |ΔRRCS|; it orders the CFR table.
Kruskal-Wallis test
A test of whether several groups differ in their typical values, using ranks instead of raw values; H is the test statistic, and a small p means a difference this large would be unlikely if the groups were alike.
Recurrence
How many receptors show an above-threshold change at the same generic-numbered position.

Links and files

UniProt
The reference protein database; each report links to the receptor's UniProt entry by its accession, such as P07550. Learn more
CSV (comma-separated values)
A plain table file that opens in any spreadsheet program; every table here can be exported as one.
