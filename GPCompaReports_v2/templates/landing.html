How to read a GPCompaRe contact

RRCS assigns a contact score to a residue pair from the interatomic distances within a structure. The same pair is scored in the active model and in the inactive model, and the difference is ΔRRCS: active minus inactive.

I121 and F282 as they appear in the ADRB2 report
Residue 1I121 3.40x40, TM3
Residue 2F282 6.44x44, TM6
Active RRCS1.79
Inactive RRCS4.69
ΔRRCS−2.90
ADRB2 threshold1.97, so this contact passes
Rank in ADRB234th of 700 contact pairs by |ΔRRCS|, so not the largest change in this receptor

Blue: stronger in the inactive model. Red: stronger in the active model.

Core Functional Residues across Class A GPCRs

A receptor-level Core Functional Residue (CFR) is a residue that participates in at least one contact whose absolute ΔRRCS passes that receptor's own threshold.

No motif was selected in advance. The cross-receptor analysis recovers established parts of the DRY, NPxxY and CWxP activation machinery on its own.

{% call fine_print(summary='How the positions are counted') %}

Mapping those receptor-level CFRs onto GPCRdb generic positions gives recurrent CFR positions across the family. A position is counted as recurrent when it is identified as a CFR in at least three receptors.

That mapping needs a GPCRdb generic number. The cross-receptor recurrence statistics reported here are computed on the full GPCRdb residue maps for all 283 receptors, so they are not limited by how much of a receptor its own annotation file numbers.

What each receptor report contains

  • An interactive snake plot with selectable colour views and contact arcs
  • Contact pairs ranked by |ΔRRCS|, with the active RRCS, the inactive RRCS and the difference, up to 1,000 rows
  • The receptor's own threshold, and which contacts pass it
  • Cross-family Core Functional Residue positions mapped onto this receptor
  • GPCRdb generic numbering
  • Sequence conservation
  • gnomAD missense variation at residues in contact pairs with |ΔRRCS| ≥ 3.0, for the 279 receptors that have variant data
  • AlphaMissense pathogenicity predictions
  • Ranked tables with CSV export

Methods, data sources and references

Structures are paired active-state and inactive-state models from AlphaFold-Multistate (Heo and Feig, Proteins 2022). Contacts are scored with RRCS. Generic numbering and segment boundaries come from GPCRdb. Population variation comes from {{ data_versions.sources.gnomad.long_label }}, pathogenicity predictions from AlphaMissense (Cheng et al., Science 2023; copyright 2023 DeepMind Technologies Limited, CC BY 4.0; averaged per position here), and conservation from VarSite scores served by ProtVar. Receptor accessions come from UniProt.

  • Heo L, Feig M. Multi-state modeling of G-protein coupled receptors at experimental accuracy. Proteins 2022;90(11):1873-1885. doi 10.1002/prot.26382

  • Cheng J et al. Accurate proteome-wide missense variant effect prediction with AlphaMissense. Science 2023;381:eadg7492. doi 10.1126/science.adg7492
