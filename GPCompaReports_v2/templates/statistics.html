{# ---- C. Core Functional Residues ---- #}

Core Functional Residues

Which positions change in most receptors?

Core Functional Residues (CFRs) are one class-wide set: the {{ cfr_top_n }} generic-numbered positions with the highest CFR score, a composite of how many receptors show an above-threshold change at the position and the mean |ΔRRCS| there. {{ cfr_total_positions }} positions recur in three or more receptors; the {{ cfr_top_n }} reported here are the top of that ranking, and the report snake plots, the residue tables and search mark the same {{ cfr_top_n }}.

{% call fine_print(summary="What are Core Functional Residues (CFRs)? How they are computed, and where the cross-receptor numbers come from") %}

Residues that carry a GPCRdb generic number are mapped onto that shared position across the {{ total_gpcrs }} receptors. A position is flagged as a CFR if it appears in the above-threshold contact list of many receptors, meaning that position repeatedly participates in active-inactive contact remodeling across the family. The mapping used here comes from the full GPCRdb residue maps for all {{ total_gpcrs }} receptors.

Positions are ranked by a composite score of (1) how many receptors show an above-threshold change at that position (recurrence) and (2) the average magnitude of change (mean |ΔRRCS|). High-ranking positions mark generic-numbered sites that recur across receptors and may reflect conserved components of Class A GPCR activation-associated contact networks.

The ranked list is cut at the top {{ cfr_top_n }} positions. That cutoff is a reporting threshold, not a definition: {{ cfr_total_positions }} positions meet the CFR criterion of recurring in at least three receptors. A position ranked below {{ cfr_top_n }} is still a recurrent CFR position, it just falls outside the subset reported here.

Where these cross-receptor numbers come from. Cross-receptor recurrence analyses are limited to residues assigned GPCRdb generic numbers. Residues without one, largely in loops and terminal regions, cannot be aligned to a shared position across receptors and take no part in these statistics. The paper's recurrence counts (tables below) are computed on the full GPCRdb residue maps for all {{ total_gpcrs }} receptors. The site-layer atlas uses each receptor's own GPCRdb numbering from the Studio packs; with it 49 of the {{ cfr_top_n }} positions match the submitted receptor counts exactly, and 8.48x48 counts one receptor more (GPR4, whose batch annotation belonged to GPR42). Individual receptor reports are unchanged and keep their own report-generation data.
