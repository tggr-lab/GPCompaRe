C. Core Functional Residues (CFRs)

Positions that repeatedly show above-threshold ΔRRCS changes across many GPCRs, suggesting recurrent activation-associated contact remodeling.

What are Core Functional Residues (CFRs)?

Residues that carry a GPCRdb generic number are mapped onto that shared position across the {{ total_gpcrs }} receptors. A position is flagged as a CFR if it appears in the above-threshold contact list of many receptors, meaning that position repeatedly participates in active-inactive contact remodeling across the family. The mapping used here comes from the full GPCRdb residue maps for all {{ total_gpcrs }} receptors.

Positions are ranked by a composite score of (1) how many receptors show an above-threshold change at that position (recurrence) and (2) the average magnitude of change (mean |ΔRRCS|). High-ranking positions mark generic-numbered sites that recur across receptors and may reflect conserved components of Class A GPCR activation-associated contact networks.

The ranked list below is cut at the top {{ cfr_top_n }} positions. That cutoff is a reporting threshold, not a definition: {{ cfr_total_positions }} positions meet the CFR criterion of recurring in at least three receptors. A position ranked below {{ cfr_top_n }} is still a recurrent CFR position, it just falls outside the subset reported here.

Where these cross-receptor numbers come from. Cross-receptor recurrence analyses are limited to residues assigned GPCRdb generic numbers. Residues without one, largely in loops and terminal regions, cannot be aligned to a shared position across receptors and take no part in these statistics. Recurrence statistics on this page are computed on the full GPCRdb residue maps for all 283 receptors. The per-receptor annotation used to build individual report pages numbers only residues that took part in a high-magnitude contact, so it yields lower recurrence counts. Individual receptor reports are unchanged and keep their own report-generation data.
