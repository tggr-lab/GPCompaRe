    How to read this

    One glyph per receptor. Zoomed out, a glyph is a dot in its ligand group's color. Zoomed in, it shows the receptor's seven transmembrane helices as discs, laid out as the bundle is seen from the extracellular side (TM1 to TM7 counterclockwise, TM3 near the center), with helix 8 as a small bar. The layout is a schematic, the same for every receptor, not the model's coordinates.

    Discs and lines. Disc size is the summed |ΔRRCS| of the receptor's above-threshold contacts on that helix; disc color is their net sum. Lines join two helices that share above-threshold contacts, colored by the net sum and drawn thicker for larger sums. Every glyph uses the same scale, so glyphs can be compared directly.

    Sign. Red: stronger in the active model. Blue: stronger in the inactive model.

    Threshold.

    Models. Each receptor has one predicted active model and one predicted inactive model from GPCRdb. The values describe these models, not measured structures.

    Layouts. Families follows GPCRdb's family classification, grouped by ligand type; it is not a phylogeny. Similarity places receptors that are alike under the chosen rule near each other, and Tree shows that rule's similarity tree; both are browsing aids, not classifications. Ranked sorts receptors by one number. Fingerprints shows every receptor's net ΔRRCS at each of the 298 generic positions shared by at least a quarter of the receptors.

    Similarity and groups. Contact-pattern similarity compares which residue contacts change between the two models, and in which direction, for every pair of generic positions (1 = the same contacts change the same way, 0 = unrelated). The intracellular and extracellular rules use only the contacts on that side; the sequence rule uses residue identity at the shared generic positions. Each rule has its own map, links, nearest neighbors and similarity tree, and the Groups slider cuts that tree into groups: a browsing aid, not a classification. Under the sequence rule the Tree layout shows a phylogenetic tree (maximum likelihood, FastTree, over the GPCRdb generic residue positions), downloadable in Newick format; under the contact rules it shows a similarity tree (hierarchical clustering), not a phylogeny.

    Position lens. The bars under the map count, for each generic position, the receptors with an above-threshold contact change there: up (red) when the residue's net change is stronger in the active model, down (blue) when stronger in the inactive model. Hover or drag along it to color every receptor by that one position; click to keep it.
