Core Functional Residues

Which positions change in most receptors?

Core Functional Residues (CFRs) are one class-wide set: the 50 generic-numbered positions with the highest CFR score, a composite of how many receptors show an above-threshold change at the position and the mean |ΔRRCS| there. 368 positions recur in three or more receptors; the 50 reported here are the top of that ranking, and the report snake plots, the residue tables and search mark the same 50.

What are Core Functional Residues (CFRs)? How they are computed, and where the cross-receptor numbers come from

Residues that carry a GPCRdb generic number are mapped onto that shared position across the 283 receptors. A position is flagged as a CFR if it appears in the above-threshold contact list of many receptors, meaning that position repeatedly participates in active-inactive contact remodeling across the family. The mapping used here comes from the full GPCRdb residue maps for all 283 receptors.

Positions are ranked by a composite score of (1) how many receptors show an above-threshold change at that position (recurrence) and (2) the average magnitude of change (mean |ΔRRCS|). High-ranking positions mark generic-numbered sites that recur across receptors and may reflect conserved components of Class A GPCR activation-associated contact networks.

The ranked list is cut at the top 50 positions. That cutoff is a reporting threshold, not a definition: 368 positions meet the CFR criterion of recurring in at least three receptors. A position ranked below 50 is still a recurrent CFR position, it just falls outside the subset reported here.

Where these cross-receptor numbers come from. Cross-receptor recurrence analyses are limited to residues assigned GPCRdb generic numbers. Residues without one, largely in loops and terminal regions, cannot be aligned to a shared position across receptors and take no part in these statistics. The paper's recurrence counts (tables below) are computed on the full GPCRdb residue maps for all 283 receptors. The site-layer atlas uses each receptor's own GPCRdb numbering from the Studio packs; with it 49 of the 50 positions match the submitted receptor counts exactly, and 8.48x48 counts one receptor more (GPR4, whose batch annotation belonged to GPR42). Individual receptor reports are unchanged and keep their own report-generation data.

Segment order for every chart on this page. Helices first: TM1 to TM7 and H8, then the loops, then the termini. Sequence: along the chain. The models are most reliable in the helices. The choice is remembered in this browser.

Variants

Are variants at CFR positions predicted pathogenic?

This AlphaMissense comparison uses 276 receptors, excluding GPR3, GPR4 and XCR1 because of mismatched annotations in the original inputs. The corrected analysis places alleles on model residue numbering.

AlphaMissense pathogenicity at the top 50 positions by CFR score

Among decisive AlphaMissense predictions, 66.9% of substitutions at the top 50 positions by CFR score were classified as pathogenic, compared with 43.6% at other generic-numbered positions: odds ratio 2.61 (95% CI 2.44 to 2.79). After stratification by conservation the odds ratio is 1.54 (95% CI 1.42 to 1.67). This association supports the CFR score as a feature for prioritizing variants. It does not establish pathogenicity.

Counts, denominators and method

Ambiguous predictions are shown in the chart but excluded from the odds ratio and the percentages. Top 50 positions by CFR score: 7,268 scored substitutions, 6,155 decisive (4,117 pathogenic, 2,038 benign) and 1,113 ambiguous. Other generic-numbered positions: 11,040 scored substitutions, 9,369 decisive (4,085 pathogenic, 5,284 benign) and 1,671 ambiguous. The comparison group is every other generic-numbered position, which includes recurrent positions ranked below the cutoff.

Source: Supplementary Figure S4 and Table S7 (unique substitutions; Woolf 95% CI; Mantel-Haenszel odds ratio over conservation quintiles of eligible decisive calls, Robins-Breslow-Greenland 95% CI). Alleles are the gnomAD v4.1.0 records behind the paper (batch layer).

More site-layer charts: conservation against contact change (a null result)

The conservation chart uses the site's own variant layer, which covers 279 receptors. NPY6R and TAAR3 lack protein-coding missense annotations because they are annotated as pseudogene loci.
